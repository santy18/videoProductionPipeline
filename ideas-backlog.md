## Unit 1 — Love: components and styles
- **Passion vs companionate love** ★★★ — *Hook:* "The butterflies were never meant to last — and that's not a bad sign." *Angle:* Hatfield & Berscheid: intense longing early, often giving way to deep friendship and trust; what keeps both alive. *Verify:* Hatfield & Berscheid original + a review; long-term passion studies (Acevedo & Aron). *Risk:* low. *Story:* the same couple at year 1 vs year 10.
- **The 6 love styles** ★★ — *Hook:* "Which of these six kinds of lover are you?" *Angle:* eros, ludus, storge, pragma, mania, agape (Lee; Hendrick & Hendrick) as a quick, fun taxonomy; not a diagnosis. *Verify:* Lee 1973; Hendrick & Hendrick love attitudes scale. *Risk:* low. *Story:* six tiny vignettes (could use `broll` ×6).
- **Ludus: why game-players struggle to stay faithful** ★ — links to Unit 7. *Angle:* game-playing love style as a predictor of later infidelity. *Verify:* the actual studies; effect size. *Risk:* med.
- **Falling in love = growing** ★★★ — *Hook:* "You don't just fall for a person. You fall for who you become with them." *Angle:* Aron & Aron self-expansion model; why novelty together keeps couples close. *Verify:* Aron & Aron 1986; "exciting activities" couples studies (Aron et al., 2000). *Risk:* low. *Story:* a couple trying new things.
- **The 4 things that make us attracted (and the boring truth)** ★★ — *Hook:* "Attraction is less romantic than you think." *Angle:* proximity, similarity, familiarity, attractiveness, reciprocity. *Verify:* Festinger (proximity), mere exposure (Zajonc), reciprocity of liking studies. *Risk:* low-med (similarity effects are debated).
- **The 8 kinds of love, rapid-fire** ★★ — all eight Sternberg types incl. nonlove, liking, infatuation, empty love (sequel to the finished video). *Risk:* low.
- **Fatuous love: why whirlwind marriages feel real** ★★ — passion + commitment without intimacy. *Risk:* low (frame as Sternberg's model, not a prediction).
- **Why long marriages fade to "companionate"** ★★ — passion drifts down while intimacy/commitment stay. *Risk:* low-med.
- **Empty love and arranged marriages** ★ — commitment-only love; careful not to stereotype cultures. *Verify:* Sternberg; cross-cultural arranged-marriage satisfaction research. *Risk:* med.

## Unit 2 — Attachment
- **Attachment styles are not four boxes** ★★★ — *Hook:* "That attachment-style quiz you took? It's oversimplified." *Angle:* research uses two dimensions, anxiety and avoidance (Brennan, Clark & Shaver; Fraley), not four types. *Verify:* ECR scale papers; Fraley's resources. *Risk:* low.
- **Where it began: the Strange Situation** ★★ — Bowlby, Ainsworth, the 20-minute experiment with toddlers. *Verify:* Ainsworth 1978; later disorganized category (Main & Solomon). *Risk:* low. *Story:* a toddler, a parent leaving and returning.
- **Romantic love is an attachment bond** ★★ — Hazan & Shaver 1987: adults love like children bond. *Verify:* the original paper + criticisms. *Risk:* low-med.
- **Anxious vs avoidant: the pursue-withdraw dance** ★★★ — *Hook:* "She wants closeness. He wants space. Both are scared of the same thing." *Angle:* how anxiety and avoidance meet in couples. *Verify:* attachment reviews (Mikulincer & Shaver). *Risk:* low-med (avoid diagnosing). *Story:* Mia and Leo-style couple, two reactions to a fight.
- **Can your attachment style change? "Earned security"** ★★ — *Verify:* longitudinal attachment stability studies; therapy outcomes. *Risk:* med (evidence is mixed; present as "can shift").
- **Does attachment predict who cheats?** ★ — avoidance and distance vs anxiety and reassurance-seeking. *Verify:* meta-analytic evidence. *Risk:* med-high (small effects, mixed).

## Unit 3 — Biology and the brain
- **What new love does to your brain** ★★★ — *Hook:* "Early love lights up the same circuits as craving." *Angle:* Fisher/Aron/Brown fMRI: ventral tegmental area and caudate; reward and motivation. *Verify:* Aron et al. 2005; Bartels & Zeki 2000. *Risk:* med (don't claim "love is a drug"; small samples, media overreach).
- **Lust, attraction, attachment: three systems** ★★ — Fisher's model and the hormones named for each. *Verify:* Fisher's papers; note it is a framework, not settled neuroscience. *Risk:* med.
- **Oxytocin isn't "the love hormone"** ★★★ — *Hook:* "Everything you've heard about the cuddle hormone is too simple." *Angle:* what oxytocin and vasopressin do, what it doesn't. *Verify:* reviews on oxytocin replication problems. *Risk:* med (needs careful sourcing, great myth-buster).
- **Prairie voles: what they teach us — and what they don't** ★★ — *Verify:* Carter, Young; limits when applied to humans. *Risk:* med. *Story:* animated vole couple (clearly not human).
- **Why heartbreak physically hurts** ★★★ — *Hook:* "Rejection lights up pain circuits." *Angle:* social pain overlap (Eisenberger; Fisher et al. rejection scans) and addiction-like craving; nuance on how strong the overlap is. *Verify:* Eisenberger 2003; Fisher et al. 2010; criticisms. *Risk:* med.
- **Does "love is chemistry" make it less real?** ★ — essay-style video from the course's discussion question. *Risk:* low.

## Unit 5 — Keeping love alive
- **Why do some people stay? The investment model** ★★★ — *Hook:* "You can be unhappy and still stay — and happy and still leave. Here's the equation." *Angle:* Rusbult 1980: commitment = satisfaction + investments − alternatives. *Verify:* Rusbult 1980; Le & Agnew meta-analysis. *Risk:* low. *Story:* three people, same unhappiness, different outcomes.
- **Gottman's Four Horsemen** ★★★ — criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling, and the antidote for each. *Verify:* Gottman's own publications; notes on replication/measurement. *Risk:* med (famous but often overstated; be careful with "predicts divorce with 90% accuracy"-type claims, do not use them without a source).
- **Turning toward: the tiny moments that save relationships** ★★★ — Gottman's bids for connection. *Verify:* Gottman & Driver 2005. *Risk:* low-med.
- **The 5:1 ratio — is it real?** ★★ — *Hook:* "Couples need five good moments for every bad one. Or do they?" *Angle:* test the popular claim against its origin and critics. *Verify:* Gottman papers and critiques of the ratio. *Risk:* med-high (great myth-check, source carefully).
- **Positive illusions: why happy couples flatter each other in their heads** ★★ — Murray & Holmes. *Verify:* original studies. *Risk:* low.
- **The Michelangelo effect** ★★ — partners "sculpt" each other into their ideal selves. *Verify:* Drigotas et al. 1999. *Risk:* low-med.
- **Why happy people ignore attractive strangers** ★★ — derogating alternatives. *Verify:* Simpson, Gangestad & Lerma 1990; Johnson & Rusbult 1989. *Risk:* low-med.
- **Why stress hurts good couples: vulnerability-stress-adaptation** ★ — Karney & Bradbury. *Risk:* low.
- **Why a satisfied person can still cheat** ★★★ — the course's own "discuss" question; ties Unit 5 to Unit 7. *Risk:* med.
