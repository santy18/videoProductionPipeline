# Stage 7 — Publish description (TikTok + Instagram)

Write `07_publish/description.md` for every video, after the render. One caption works for both platforms.
TikTok and Instagram are search engines now: people type questions into the search bar, and the caption's words
(plus on-screen text and the spoken audio) decide whether the video is found. So the description must carry the
right **keywords**, written as natural sentences, never as a stuffed list.

## Format (in this order)
1. **Line 1 – hook + primary keyword** (≤ 125 chars, shown before the "more" cut-off). Say what the viewer gets, in the
   words they would search: e.g. "The psychology of love: every kind of love is a mix of 3 ingredients 💛".
2. **2–3 sentences of substance** that naturally contain 4–6 *secondary keywords* (see below) and name the key idea,
   researcher or fact. Mention the year/researcher when it's a study (it also signals credibility).
3. **One comment-bait question** that matches the video's ending ("Which one is yours? 👇"). Comments boost reach.
4. **Source line** (CLAUDE.md rule): "Source: Sternberg (1986), Psychological Review." Never invent a source.
5. **CTA**: "Follow for more" (channel default).
6. **Exactly 5 hashtags**, last line.

## Keywords: how to choose
- Start from the idea title and `idea.json`. Write down 8–10 phrases someone would plausibly type: the topic itself
  ("triangular theory of love"), the question it answers ("why do relationships fade"), the category
  ("psychology of love", "relationship advice", "types of love"), the audience ("couples", "dating"), and the researcher.
- Use the **primary keyword in line 1** and 4–6 others in the body. Repeating one keyword twice is fine; more is stuffing.
- Only use keywords the video actually delivers. No bait-and-switch terms and no trending-but-unrelated tags: it gets the
  video suppressed and breaks the "never clickbait-false" tone rule.
- Prefer specific long-tail phrases over generic ones ("attachment styles in relationships" beats "love").
- Say it in the video too: the primary keyword should appear in the spoken narration or on-screen text of scene 1–2.

## Hashtags (exactly 5)
- 1 broad niche tag (`#psychology`), 1 broad topic tag (`#relationships`), 2 specific topic tags
  (`#lovepsychology`, `#psychologyfacts`), 1 audience/context tag (`#couples`).
- Mix reach and relevance; avoid banned/spammy tags (#fyp-style tags add no search value).
- Hashtags go on the last line, separated from the text by a blank line.

## Template
```
<Hook line with primary keyword> <emoji>

<2–3 sentences with 4–6 secondary keywords, naming the researcher/year/idea.>

<Comment-bait question> 👇

Source: <Author> (<year>), <Journal/Book>. Follow for more!

#tag1 #tag2 #tag3 #tag4 #tag5
```
Sensitive topics (infidelity, trauma, jealousy as control): no blame, no diagnosing viewers; add "If this is happening to you, support exists." where relevant.
